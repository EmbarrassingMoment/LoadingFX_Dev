## [Unreleased]

### Added

- Linux in every module's `PlatformAllowList`. Compile-checked with the UE 5.5 and 5.8 cross-compile toolchains (Development and Shipping); not yet run on Linux.
- `AGENTS.md` in the plugin folder: a usage guide for AI coding agents (public API, settings, bundled materials and their parameters, the map travel pattern, verification, pitfalls), with a `CLAUDE.md` that imports it for Claude Code. Both are listed in `FilterPlugin.ini`, so they ship in the Fab package. The README explains how to point an agent at it.
- Material parameters are grouped in the material instance editor: `Appearance` (color and opacities), `Shape` (layout and sizes) and `Motion` (speed and timing), plus `Input` for `Progress` on the progress bar material. Parameter names follow one vocabulary across the library (`Radius`, `Thickness`, `Spacing`, `DotRadiusMin` / `DotRadiusMax`, `OpacityMin` / `OpacityMax`, `TailDelay`, ...).
- Initial project setup: the `LoadingFX_Dev` sample project (UE 5.5) and the `LoadingFX` plugin skeleton with the `LoadingFX`, `LoadingFXLoader` and `LoadingFXBridge` runtime modules.
- The first spinner materials (plus `MF_GLSLMod` and `MF_SdRoundBox2D`), generated by Python builder scripts with the local-only `DevMaterialTools` plugin. `M_LoadingDotRing`: a ring of dots that shrink toward the tail, turning clockwise (the default loading icon). `M_LoadingDotGrid`: eight dots on a square that a pulse swells one after another, clockwise. `M_LoadingBoxes`: four rounded boxes that brighten in turn.
- `M_LoadingSpokes`: the classic radial-bar spinner (rounded bars, a fade that runs clockwise). Its anti-aliasing width comes from the screen derivatives of the distance field (`fwidth`) rather than from the widget size.
- `M_LoadingArc`: a single rounded arc that stretches and shrinks while it turns. `Speed` sets the steady rotation and `StretchSpeed` the stretch cycle; `ArcMin` / `ArcMax` set the shortest and longest arc in turns.
- `M_LoadingComet`: a round head running clockwise around a ring, with a tail that thins and fades out over one full turn. The tail fades in opacity, so it stays clean on light backgrounds.
- `M_LoadingDotRow`: a row of dots (three by default) that swell and brighten one after another from left to right, then rest. It suits wide and small places such as buttons.
- `M_LoadingDualArc`: two pairs of opposite arcs on an outer and an inner ring, turning in opposite directions, in one color. `InnerSpeedRatio` sets the inner ring's speed relative to `Speed`.
- `M_LoadingSegments`: a ring of rounded segments that light up one by one clockwise from 12 o'clock, then go out in the same order. Unlit segments stay faintly visible (`OpacityMin`); `Gap` sets the space between segments.
- `M_LoadingTimer`: a pie inside a thin ring that fills clockwise from 12 o'clock, then empties in the same direction, like a timer. `RingOpacity` fades the ring independently.
- `M_LoadingHourglass` (with `MF_SdSegment2D`): sand runs from the top bulb into the bottom one with a thin falling stream, then the glass turns over clockwise and starts again. It shrinks a little while it turns, so it stays close to its resting size.
- `M_LoadingBounce` (with `MF_SdEllipse2D`): a ball that bounces on a floor and flattens while it touches it, over a shadow that shrinks and fades as the ball rises.
- `M_LoadingRipple`: rings that start at the center, slow down as they spread out and fade away, like ripples on water. `Count` sets how many rings are on screen at a time and `Easing` how much they slow down; a negative `Speed` makes them close in instead.
- `M_LoadingEqualizer`: a row of rounded bars (five by default) that grow and shrink around the center line one after another from left to right, like an audio equalizer. It shares its timing parameters with `M_LoadingDotRow` (`Stagger`, `PulseWidth`).
- `M_LoadingGooey`: droplets (three by default) bud off a round core like liquid, drift outward while the whole shape turns clockwise, then melt back into the core. `Smoothness` sets how gooey the joins are.
- `M_LoadingMorph`: a rounded square that turns a quarter clockwise, rounding off into a circle halfway through the turn, then pauses. `Roundness` sets how round it gets and `TurnFraction` how long the pause is.
- Loading bars. The bar materials span the widget's width (`MF_SpinnerCoords` gained an `Extent` output, the widget's half extents in `P` units) and keep the same inset on every side, so a bar is a pill that fills a wide widget such as 320x16. `M_LoadingBarSlide`: an indeterminate bar, a segment that grows from the left, runs right and shrinks away (`TailDelay` sets its length). `M_LoadingBarStripes`: an indeterminate bar with diagonal stripes running right (`Period`, `Duty`). `M_LoadingBarFill`: a determinate bar filled to its `Progress` parameter (0 to 1), with a sheen that sweeps over the filled part (`SheenWidth`, `SheenStrength`; visible on a colored `PrimaryColor`). All three share `Thickness` (1.6 by default, 2 fills the widget) and `TrackOpacity`.
- `M_LoadingLine`: an indeterminate progress bar. A bar grows from the left end of a faint horizontal track and leaves at the right end, with no pause between sweeps. `TailDelay` sets how long the bar gets, `TrackOpacity` fades the track, and a negative `Speed` runs it from right to left.
- `M_LoadingChase`: dots (five by default, up to eight) that chase each other clockwise around a ring. They slow down and melt into one blob at 12 o'clock, then pull apart as they speed through 6 o'clock. `Smoothness` sets how gooey the joins are (0 keeps the dots separate), `Easing` how much they slow down and `Stagger` how closely they follow each other.
- `MF_SpinnerCoords` and the material contract: every bundled spinner uses centered, aspect-corrected coordinates, real time (`Time` with Ignore Pause), multiplies `VertexColor` into its output and shares a default footprint (ring layouts touch a circle of radius 0.8, grid layouts a square of half-width 0.72).
- `ULoadingFXSettings` (Project Settings > Plugins > LoadingFX): loading icon material, size, placement, tint, z-order, show delay and minimum display time. The effective material is added to every cook through `FGameDelegates::ModifyCookDelegate`. The z-order defaults to 10010, above full-screen overlays at 10000 such as TransitionFX's widget-layer transitions, so the icon shows over a screen held covered while loading.
- `ULoadingFXIconSubsystem`: reference-counted loading icon (`Acquire` / `Release` handles, optional auto-release when an owner object is destroyed, `OnVisibilityChanged`). The icon is viewport content, so it survives map travel, and while it is visible a blocking map load is rendered through MoviePlayer with the icon as the loading screen. MoviePlayer is unavailable in the editor and PIE, so the icon freezes there during blocking loads. Console: `LoadingFX.Icon.Show`, `LoadingFX.Icon.Hide`, `LoadingFX.Icon.Dump`.
- Show delay and minimum display time for the loading icon, so short loads do not make it flicker. The icon appears only once the first request has been active for `IconShowDelay` (0.5 s by default; releasing every request before that shows nothing), and once it is up it stays for at least `IconMinimumDisplayTime` (1 s by default), also through `ReleaseAll`; an `Acquire` in the meantime keeps it up without hiding it in between. Both count in real time, so they run while the game is paused and through blocking loads. A blocking map load that starts within the delay is still rendered through MoviePlayer, and the icon appears on that screen when the delay is over. `IsIconVisible` and `OnVisibilityChanged` follow what is on screen; `GetRequestCount` counts the requests.
- The loading icon subsystem preloads the icon material asynchronously when it initializes. Loading the material also starts precaching its PSOs, so showing the icon for the first time neither loads the material on the game thread nor compiles its pipeline states. An `Acquire` made before the preload has finished waits for that load, so the icon still appears at once.
- `SLoadingFXSpinner`: Slate widget that draws a spinner material at a given size and tint.
- `ULoadingFXSpinner` (UMG "LoadingFX Spinner", palette category LoadingFX): material (empty = the project's loading icon material), color and opacity, size, `SetOpacity`, and `GetDynamicMaterial` for per-widget parameter changes such as `PrimaryColor` or `Speed`. Its material picker, and the loading icon material picker in Project Settings, list the bundled materials even while the Content Browser's **Show Plugin Content** setting is off.
- `ULoadingFXProgressBar` (UMG "LoadingFX Progress Bar") and `SLoadingFXProgressBar`: draw a determinate bar material (empty = `M_LoadingBarFill`, added to every cook) through a dynamic instance and write its `Progress`. An increase is shown over `SmoothingTime` (0.25 s by default), a decrease at once. Connect Load Assets Async's On Progress to Set Progress. The dynamic instance never replaces `Material`, so a widget blueprint can be saved with the material it was given.
- `ULoadingFXLoaderSubsystem` (`LoadingFXLoader` module): loads soft object and soft class references in the background, with progress and cancel, or synchronously. The loaded assets stay in memory until the load is released or its owner object is destroyed (the Blueprint nodes default the owner to the calling object). Blueprint: the Load Assets Async node (On Progress / On Completed / On Canceled, cancelable), Load Assets Sync, Release Loaded Assets, Release All Loaded Assets. Progress counts requested assets. Console: `LoadingFX.Loader.Dump`.
- Every load can ask for the loading icon (`bShowLoadingIcon`): the `LoadingFXBridge` module shows the icon while the load runs. A sync load also keeps an icon that is already requested animating while the game thread is blocked (on a black screen, through MoviePlayer).
- `ULoadingFXIconSubsystem::BeginBlockingSection` / `EndBlockingSection` and `FLoadingFXBlockingScope` (C++): keep the loading icon animating through any work that blocks the game thread, if the icon is requested.

### Fixed

- The loading icon is drawn on the MoviePlayer loading screen in uncooked runs (`-game`, Standalone Game) when it was never on screen before the blocking load, for example when it is acquired right before Open Level ([#5](https://github.com/EmbarrassingMoment/LoadingFX_Dev/issues/5)). Uncooked processes compile a material's shaders only when it is first drawn, and only the game thread takes the results in, so an icon first drawn on the loading screen stayed blank until the load was over (also on the first run after the plugin was added). The icon subsystem now compiles the icon material's shaders when it loads it, which takes a few tens of ms at startup in uncooked runs (a second or two the first time). Cooked builds were not affected and skip it.
